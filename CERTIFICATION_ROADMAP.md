Gate 4 — Regulatory / Certification

The actual certification pathway must be determined by:

- jurisdiction;
- vehicle category;
- vehicle manufacturer;
- system function;
- whether the system is safety-critical;
- whether the system directly controls vehicle functions;
- communications technology;
- update mechanism;
- AI functionality.

Potential regulatory frameworks may include:

United States

Review applicable:

- NHTSA requirements;
- Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards;
- cybersecurity guidance;
- communications requirements;
- privacy requirements;
- state-specific requirements.

Taiwan

Review applicable:

- vehicle safety requirements;
- type approval;
- communications requirements;
- personal-data requirements;
- cybersecurity requirements;
- product liability.

People's Republic of China

Review applicable:

- vehicle safety/type approval;
- automotive data requirements;
- cybersecurity;
- data security;
- personal-information protection;
- algorithm / AI requirements;
- cross-border data rules.

Switzerland

Review applicable:

- Swiss vehicle requirements;
- Swiss data protection;
- communications;
- product liability;
- applicable international vehicle regulations.

European Union / UNECE

Review applicable:

- EU vehicle type approval;
- GDPR;
- EU AI Act;
- cybersecurity;
- software updates;
- UNECE regulations.

In applicable vehicle programs, UN Regulation No. 155 concerns cybersecurity and cybersecurity management systems.

UN Regulation No. 156 concerns software updates and software-update management systems.

---

Important Principle

A GitHub repository is not a certificate.

A software demonstration is not a vehicle approval.

An AI model is not automatically an autonomous-driving system.

A business plan is not an investment offering.

A contract is not a substitute for mandatory regulatory approval.

Certification must be obtained through the applicable regulatory authority, type-approval authority, certification body or testing laboratory.
